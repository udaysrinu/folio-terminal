Someone shared a stock basket with you

What to do with it · about 2 minutes

A basket is a list of stocks with a target percentage for each. The file you were sent holds those percentages — not a number of shares — so it works no matter how much money you're putting in. You could invest ₹50,000 or ₹5,00,000 from the same file.

What to do

1
Open the workbench

Click the button below. It's a single web page — nothing to install, no sign-up, and it never sees your broker account.

Open the Basket Workbench →
2
Load the basket

Drag the .json file onto the page, or paste the text if it was sent to you in a message. You'll immediately see how many stocks it holds, how concentrated it is, and the minimum amount it needs.

3
Type what you want to invest

You get an exact shopping list — how many shares of each stock. If your amount is below the basket's minimum, the page will say so and suggest workable amounts, because a smaller amount would silently drop the expensive stocks and leave you with something different.

4
Place the orders

Either Copy as text and enter them in your broker by hand, or — if the sender set up one-click ordering — press Place in Kite, which opens Kite with everything pre-filled for you to review and confirm. Nothing is ever placed without you approving it.

Already own the basket?

↻
Drop your holdings file in Step 5

Download your holdings from your broker (Zerodha: Console → Holdings → download) and drop it on the page. It compares what you own against the basket's targets and tells you exactly what to buy or sell to line them up again.

Questions people ask

Does this see my broker account or my money?

No. It has no login, no password, no API access, and no server. Everything happens inside your browser tab. You place the orders yourself.

Why does it need me to type prices sometimes?

A page with no server can't look up live prices, and browsers block calls to exchange websites. If the basket arrived without prices, type them from your broker app — they're only used to convert percentages into share counts.

Why can't I invest a smaller amount?

Indian exchanges only allow whole shares. If one stock in the basket costs ₹10,000 and is meant to be 7% of your money, you need roughly ₹1,50,000 before a single share of it fits. Below that you'd own a different, more lopsided basket.

Will my share counts match the sender's?

Almost certainly not, and that's correct. You match their percentages, not their share counts. Share counts depend on how much each of you invested and what prices you paid.

This is not advice. Whoever shared this basket is not your financial adviser, and neither is this page. Check every order before you confirm it, and only invest what you've decided to invest yourself.
